Founded in 2019, the company applies advanced artificial intelligence and deep learning techniques to financial markets, focusing primarily on price prediction and automated trading signal generation.

FinGenesis distinguishes itself through a research-intensive approach to market prediction. Unlike traditional statistical models that rely on rigid algorithms, the company develops AI systems that learn from market patterns and adapt to changing conditions. Their technology stack includes deep learning architectures, reinforcement learning, convolutional networks, and transformer models similar in sophistication to large language models like ChatGPT, but specifically designed for financial time-series analysis.

\subsection*{Core Product Offerings}

FinGenesis organizes its AI capabilities into distinct product lines:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assist}: Short-term price prediction models that forecast market movements in the near term, helping traders anticipate price changes.
    
    \item \textbf{Advance}: Signal generation systems that produce actionable buy/sell recommendations based on detected market patterns.
    
    \item \textbf{Augment/Exceed}: Advanced portfolio management and risk control systems that provide comprehensive trading strategy optimization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Technology and Approach}

The company's AI infrastructure processes real-time market data across multiple instruments, with particular focus on XAUUSD (Gold/USD). Models operate across various timeframes from 1-minute to 15-minute intervals with prediction horizons ranging from 30 minutes to several hours.

FinGenesis emphasizes an "AI + human" philosophy: their systems assist traders rather than fully automating decisions. This approach combines machine intelligence with human expertise, allowing traders to leverage AI insights while maintaining strategic control.

The company's technical advantages include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous model updating}: Regular retraining and validation to prevent overfitting and maintain accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Speed}: Analysis and prediction generation 10-20 times faster than traditional methods.
    \item \textbf{Robustness}: Stress-testing under various market conditions to ensure reliability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Infrastructure and Operations}

FinGenesis operates a cloud-native infrastructure on AWS, utilizing:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Kubernetes for container orchestration
    \item GitLab for version control and CI/CD pipelines
    \item Automated deployment systems for continuous model updates
    \item Prometheus and Grafana for monitoring

The company maintains a lean team of approximately 10 specialists, including data scientists, machine learning engineers, and DevOps professionals. This structure enables rapid iteration and maintains the flexibility required in dynamic financial markets.

With 15+ production models deployed and continuously monitored, FinGenesis combines technical sophistication with practical trading applications. The company's mission is clear: use real intelligence not just algorithms to optimize trading decisions and deliver reliable market predictions.

\section{Problem Statement}

In the context of AI-driven algorithmic trading, managing and automating infrastructure efficiently is a critical operational challenge. FinGenesis's engineering team faced significant obstacles in maintaining visibility and control over their complex, distributed infrastructure.

The primary problems identified were:

\textbf{Lack of Centralization}: Infrastructure monitoring and control tools were scattered across multiple platforms. Engineers had to navigate between Grafana for system metrics, GitLab for repository and pipeline management, AWS Console for cloud resources, and manual communication channels for coordination. This fragmentation created information silos and prevented a holistic view of system health.

\textbf{Manual and Time-Consuming Processes}: Routine infrastructure operations required repetitive manual steps. Checking deployment status, triggering CI/CD pipelines, and verifying model performance metrics all involved context switching and sequential actions across different tools. These manual processes increased workload, reduced productivity, and introduced the risk of human error particularly critical in time-sensitive trading operations where delays can have financial consequences.

\textbf{Limited Visibility and Collaboration}: Internal engineering staff, stakeholders, and potential clients lacked a unified interface for accessing infrastructure data. Data scientists needed quick insights into model performance, DevOps engineers required deployment status information, and management needed high-level system health overviews but each had to piece together information from disparate sources. This limited transparency hindered collaboration and slowed decision-making.

\textbf{Scalability Challenges}: As FinGenesis's infrastructure grew adding more repositories, deploying additional models, and expanding monitoring requirements the complexity of managing multiple disconnected systems increased exponentially. The existing approach did not scale effectively, and response times degraded as the number of managed components grew (evidenced by GitLab data loading taking over 60 seconds for 40+ repositories).

The \textbf{Infrastructure Command Center} was designed to address all these challenges by providing a unified, performance-optimized platform that centralizes infrastructure management, automates routine operations, and delivers actionable insights across monitoring, deployment, and MLOps domains.

\section{Study of Existing Solutions}

\subsection{Solutions Currently Used by the Company}

Before the implementation of the Infrastructure Command Center project, \textit{FinGenesis} relied on conventional infrastructure management methods, primarily based on established third-party tools and manual processes. While functional, these mechanisms presented several notable limitations that hindered the fluidity and efficiency of infrastructure operations.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fragmented monitoring systems} : Existing tools like Grafana and AWS Console only provided visibility into specific aspects of the infrastructure. System metrics were isolated from deployment status, requiring engineers to manually correlate information across multiple platforms. This gap resulted in incomplete understanding of overall system health, complicated troubleshooting, and increased the time needed to diagnose and resolve issues.
    
    \item \textbf{Manual and labor-intensive deployment management} : Each infrastructure operation whether deploying a new version, restarting a service, or merging code changes required manual intervention through the GitLab web interface. This procedure was not only time-consuming but also prone to human error, such as triggering the wrong pipeline or missing critical deployment steps. Consequently, deployment processes became bottlenecks, losing reliability and slowing down release cycles.
    
    \item \textbf{Insufficient integration and accessibility} : To access infrastructure information or perform management tasks, team members often had to navigate between multiple platforms, each requiring specific permissions and technical knowledge. This disconnect between different tools fragmented the workflow and limited access to critical information. The direct consequence was reduced productivity and increased dependency on specific engineers with the necessary expertise and credentials.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{1cm}

\subsection{Analysis of the Existing System}
In-depth examination of the methods in place revealed several structural deficiencies that hindered operational efficiency and overall infrastructure quality:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Limited visibility and transparency} : In the current context, critical infrastructure information such as deployment status, pipeline health, or model performance metrics was scattered across disconnected platforms. The absence of a unified dashboard prevented stakeholders from obtaining a comprehensive overview, increasing the risk of delayed incident response and uninformed decision-making.
    
    \item \textbf{Scalability issues and performance degradation} : Due to the reliance on multiple separate tools and manual processes, managing infrastructure became increasingly complex as FinGenesis grew. When operations were performed across 40+ repositories, response times degraded significantly (GitLab data loading exceeded 60 seconds). These incidents forced teams to spend valuable time waiting for information or repeating tasks, considerably slowing operational cycles.
    
    \item \textbf{Poor user experience and limited accessibility} : Navigation across infrastructure management tools was dispersed and non-intuitive. Critical information was difficult to locate, connections between related services were often unclear, and presentation formats varied between platforms. This complexity increased the time needed to understand the overall infrastructure state and reduced accessibility, especially for non-technical stakeholders or new team members.
    
    \item \textbf{Lack of automation and workflow visualization} : The tools used did not provide automated orchestration of common tasks or visual representation of infrastructure workflows. The absence of clear standards for infrastructure operations resulted in inconsistent processes and limited the team's ability to efficiently leverage available automation capabilities.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Solution}

To address the limitations and inefficiencies of the existing infrastructure management approach, the \textbf{Infrastructure Command Center} (also referred to as \textbf{Infra App}) was developed as a comprehensive, centralized solution.

The Infrastructure Command Center is a unified web application that consolidates monitoring, deployment control, and workflow automation into a single, accessible platform. Rather than requiring engineers and stakeholders to navigate between Grafana, AWS Console, GitLab, and manual communication channels, the platform provides one integrated dashboard where all critical infrastructure operations can be performed.

\subsection{Core Capabilities}

The solution delivers three primary functional areas:

\textbf{Visual Workflow Representation}: The platform provides an interactive visualization of FinGenesis's infrastructure workflows, showing how services connect and interact. This visual approach replaces abstract documentation with clear, real-time diagrams that help both technical and non-technical users understand system architecture at a glance.

\textbf{Repository and Deployment Management}: All GitLab repositories are accessible through a centralized control panel with advanced filtering, search, and sorting capabilities. Common operations such as triggering deployments, restarting services, managing merge requests, and monitoring pipeline status can be executed directly from the interface without switching contexts or requiring direct GitLab access.

\textbf{MLOps Monitoring and Model Performance Tracking}: The platform integrates real-time model performance metrics, displaying accuracy trends, prediction quality, and drift detection across multiple timeframes and prediction horizons. This gives data scientists and engineers immediate visibility into model health without needing to query separate monitoring systems.

\subsection{Design Philosophy}

The Infrastructure Command Center was designed with two key principles:

\textbf{Simplicity for all users}: The interface is intuitive enough for non-technical stakeholders such as management or clients to understand system status and access relevant information without requiring specialized training or deep technical knowledge.

\textbf{Power for technical users}: Despite its accessible design, the platform provides engineers with comprehensive control over infrastructure operations, enabling them to perform complex tasks efficiently through streamlined workflows and automation.

\subsection{Expected Benefits}

The proposed solution aims to deliver measurable improvements across several operational dimensions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduced manual overhead}: Automation of routine tasks eliminates repetitive manual steps, freeing engineers to focus on higher-value work.
    
    \item \textbf{Improved collaboration}: A unified platform ensures all team members regardless of technical background have access to the same information, facilitating better communication and decision-making.
    
    \item \textbf{Decreased dependency on specialized staff}: By centralizing and simplifying infrastructure operations, the platform reduces bottlenecks caused by reliance on specific individuals with unique access or expertise.
    
    \item \textbf{Enhanced visibility and transparency}: Real-time dashboards and visual representations provide stakeholders with immediate insight into system health, deployment status, and model performance.
    
    \item \textbf{Scalability}: The centralized architecture is designed to handle growing infrastructure complexity without degrading performance or requiring proportional increases in manual effort.
\end{itemize}

By consolidating fragmented tools into one cohesive platform, the Infrastructure Command Center transforms infrastructure management from a complex, multi-tool process into a streamlined, accessible operation that scales with organizational growth.

\section{Adopted Methodology}

To ensure structured development and consistent progress throughout the six-month internship, the Infrastructure Command Center project was implemented using the \textbf{Agile methodology}, specifically following Scrum principles adapted to a single-developer context.

\subsection{Agile Framework}

Agile methodology emphasizes iterative development, continuous feedback, and adaptive planning. Rather than attempting to build the entire platform at once, the project was divided into \textbf{9 sprints}, each lasting approximately two weeks. This approach allowed for:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incremental delivery}: Each sprint produced working features that could be tested and validated immediately.
    \item \textbf{Flexibility}: Requirements and priorities could be adjusted based on feedback and evolving organizational needs.
    \item \textbf{Risk mitigation}: Problems were identified and resolved early in the development cycle rather than accumulating until project completion.
    \item \textbf{Continuous improvement}: Lessons learned from each sprint informed planning and execution of subsequent iterations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sprint Structure}

Each two-week sprint followed a consistent pattern:

\textbf{Sprint Planning}: At the beginning of each sprint, a planning session established the scope of work to be completed. Tasks were identified, prioritized, and broken down into manageable units with clear acceptance criteria. This planning ensured focused effort on high-priority features aligned with project goals.

\textbf{Daily Standups}: Every day at 9:00 AM, a brief daily meeting was held to maintain momentum and alignment. These sessions covered three key questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item What was accomplished yesterday?
    \item What will be worked on today?
    \item Are there any blockers or obstacles?
\end{itemize}

These daily check-ins ensured transparency, identified issues early, and maintained consistent communication with stakeholders.

\textbf{Sprint Review}: At the end of each sprint, an in-person review meeting was conducted to demonstrate completed features. This session provided an opportunity to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Present working functionality to stakeholders
    \item Gather feedback on implemented features
    \item Validate that sprint objectives were met
    \item Identify any adjustments needed for future development
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Sprint Retrospective}: Following the review, a retrospective session analyzed what worked well during the sprint and what could be improved. This continuous learning process enhanced efficiency and quality in subsequent iterations.

\subsection{Tools and Tracking}

To support the Agile workflow, several organizational tools were employed:

\textbf{Notion}: Served as the central project management platform, tracking:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sprint backlogs and task assignments
    \item Progress status for each feature (To Do, In Progress, Done)
    \item Documentation and technical notes
    \item Meeting notes and decisions
\end{itemize}

\textbf{GitLab}: Beyond code versioning, GitLab's issue tracking and merge request features supported task management and code review processes.

\textbf{Communication Channels}: Regular communication with technical supervisors ensured alignment with organizational standards and technical requirements.

\subsection{Adaptation to Solo Development}

While traditional Scrum assumes team-based development, the methodology was adapted for individual work while maintaining its core benefits:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Daily standups became accountability check-ins with supervisors
    \item Sprint reviews involved demonstration to stakeholders and gathering feedback
    \item The iterative approach remained essential for managing complexity and maintaining focus
    \item Clear sprint goals prevented scope creep and ensured steady progress
\end{itemize}

This structured yet flexible approach enabled consistent delivery of high-quality features across the nine sprints, ultimately producing a comprehensive platform that met all project objectives.
